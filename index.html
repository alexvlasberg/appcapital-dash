FAQ: Apple's fiscal calendar

What is Apple's fiscal calendar?

Apple calculates App Store proceeds and payouts on its own fiscal calendar, not the regular Gregorian one. Fiscal months don't line up with calendar months — which is exactly why the Proceeds: Fiscal and Proceeds: Gregorian numbers on this dashboard differ.

Why does Apple's fiscal year have 364 days?

Each fiscal quarter runs a fixed 5-4-4 week pattern (35+28+28 days = 13 weeks), so four quarters add up to 364 days — one short of a real year. Apple corrects the drift with an extra 53rd week every 4–6 years; the last one landed at the end of fiscal Q1 2023 (December 2022).

How does Apple's fiscal calendar work?

The fiscal year starts on the last Sunday of September — FY2026 started September 28, 2025. Each quarter has three fiscal months: the first runs 35 days, the other two run 28 days each. Payouts land about 33 days after each fiscal month closes, consistently on a Thursday, shifting to the next business day around holidays.

How the payout calendar works:

  • Payouts are the actual cash that lands in our bank account — a different thing from accrued revenue.
  • App Store payouts follow the fiscal quarter's rhythm: two payouts in the quarter's first month, none in the second, one in the third.
  • App Store payouts typically arrive about 33 days after the end of the prior fiscal month, though timing can vary.
  • Stripe payouts run on a separate, configurable schedule — currently set to land in the first days of each month, for the prior month's revenue.
